**Status — 2026-09-07: drafted, EINs verified, nothing approved, nothing seeded.** This page is the reviewed manifest the [Donors plan](/guides/open-work/roadmap/donors) calls for under *Entry points and source coverage*: "Seed foundations explicitly: Ford first, then verified grantor EINs from available evidence or reviewed discovery." It follows the same protocol as the recipient alias manifest — Devin drafts, Mark approves or strikes each row, and only an approved row ever seeds.

## Why an explicit seed

The Donors lane runs whenever the 990 adapter parses a filing; its feed scan does not care how the filing got there. What causes a filing to be fetched is one of three doors:

| Door | Who opens it | Automatic |
|---|---|---|
| Company waterfall step 16: the LLM classified `company_type` as Nonprofit or School | any company the pipeline is already enriching | yes |
| `POST /v1/admin/filings/990/orgs {"ein"}` | an operator | no |
| Donors `grantor_request` job (`POST /v1/admin/donors/jobs`) | an operator | no |

Door one is incidental: it fires for nonprofits the company pipeline happens to touch for other reasons. Nobody enriches the Ford Foundation because a user asked about it — Ford's grants matter because of who receives them. And IRS filings are indexed by **grantor**, not grantee: a School's own Schedule I lists what it gave, never who gave to it. There is no query against the IRS data for "everyone who gave to Stanford." The foundation corpus only grows by someone naming foundations.

Today that someone is an operator, one EIN at a time. This list is the named set.

## What the seed is

A manifest of reviewed rows, embedded in the binary like `data/recipient_aliases.json`, and a seeder registered in `cmd/seed` as `donor-grantor-seed`:

```bash
make seed ONLY=donor-grantor-seed            # local
make seed-alloydb ONLY=donor-grantor-seed    # dev / prod through the tunnel
```

Each approved row becomes one `grantor_request` job keyed `grantor:<ein>` — the same job an operator creates by hand today, so the seeder is idempotent (a re-run lands on the same row) and everything downstream is already built: the 990 adapter fetches and parses, the Donors feed scan replays, the recipient gate resolves, the ledger recomputes. The seeder adds a manifest and roughly forty lines of Go; it adds no new path.

Volume is bounded by the existing knobs. `DONORS_MAX_INSTITUTIONS_PER_RUN` (default 25) caps how many grantor requests a tick hands to the filings lane; `FILINGS_990_MAX_FILINGS_PER_RUN` (default 100) caps fetches; `DONORS_MAX_MENTIONS_PER_RUN` (default 500) caps grant lines per replay run. Forty foundations at four filings each is 160 fetches and, judging by Ford's 14,939 lines over four filings, on the order of a few hundred thousand grant lines — every one a `donation_mention`, most `unresolved` because most recipients are not Schools. That is the corpus the plan wants; it is also why this runs after the live Universe, not before.

## How the list was drafted and verified

Candidates are private grantmaking foundations known for higher-education and research giving in the United States. Every EIN below was looked up on 2026-09-07 through the ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer API (`/nonprofits/api/v2/search.json`, exact-name query, state filter) and the top hit's EIN, legal name, city and NTEE code are reproduced as returned. Nothing on this page is from memory.

Two things the verification does **not** establish, and the reviewer should not read into it:

- **Giving volume.** ProPublica's search returns no financials. Rank and hub status come from the 990 adapter's `GrantWindow` after the fetch — the `> 200 grants in the four-year window` hub rule will put nearly every row here at weight 40.
- **School relevance.** The "why on the list" column is the drafter's reason for including the row, not a measured fact. The Donors gate decides scope at the gift: a foundation that turns out to give nothing to an allowlisted School produces mentions and no gifts, which is correct and cheap.

NTEE codes are reproduced as the IRS Business Master File carries them; several are odd (Ford as `T21` "corporate foundation", Mellon as `B82` "scholarships"). They do not matter to the lane: donor class comes from the **return type** — a 990-PF filer is a foundation — never from NTEE.

## Review protocol

Same as the alias manifest, so the two reviews look alike:

1. Mark reads each tranche row and marks it approved or struck; a struck row stays on this page with the reason so it is not re-proposed.
2. The ambiguous entities get one decision each and move into a tranche or the exclusions.
3. The approved set becomes `internal/features/donors/data/grantor_seed.json` — one row per foundation with `ein`, `display_name`, `tranche`, `qualifies_reason`, `approved`, and the manifest-level `reviewed_by` / `reviewed_at`. The seeder refuses a manifest with no reviewer.
4. Tranche 1 runs first, alone, with the run's exact outputs recorded here: fetches, parsed filings, grant lines, gifts per School, hub classifications. Tranche 2 follows only after those numbers are reviewed.

## Open questions the seed does not settle

- **Corporate foundations.** Several rows carry NTEE `T21`. The lane classifies by return type, so a 990-PF filer is `foundation` at 30/40 regardless — but the ontology has a separate `corporate` class at 35. Whether an NTEE `T21` filer should be classified `corporate` is a weight question for the edge-weights page, not for this list.
- **Foreign foundations with US filers.** A Stavros Niarchos or Wellcome US entity, if one files a 990-PF, is a foundation like any other; the question is only which entity.
- **Refresh cadence.** The 990 adapter's window is the latest four filing years; a re-request of a seeded grantor re-offers new filings. How often the seed re-runs is an operations decision after tranche 1 has run once.
