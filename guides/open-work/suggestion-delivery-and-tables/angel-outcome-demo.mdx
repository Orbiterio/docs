This is a hand-written **Outcome** demo package for the seed-raise use case: six NYC-based angel investors, all `ready`, each carrying a WHY narrative and three outreach hooks. It follows the same load path as [Create Demo Suggestions](/guides/open-work/suggestion-delivery-and-tables/create-demo-suggestions): Mark writes the content, Claude resolves every `{entity ID for …}` placeholder to a real row ID, and the package loads through `cmd/ingestpackage`.

<Warning>
**The `POST /v1/ingest/suggestions` receiver described in [Suggestion Delivery and Tables](/guides/open-work/suggestion-delivery-and-tables) was not built.** This package loads with the backend CLI. The JSON below is the documented v2 envelope; Claude handles the file conversion at load time.
</Warning>

## WHY and hooks

Each candidate's WHY is investor-facing: why this person should invest in Orbiter. The `hooks` array carries three opening lines at different angles (`personal`, `thesis`, `provocative`); each hook object follows the documented shape of `entity_id`, `entity_type`, `angle`, and `text`.

### 1. Laurel Touby

**WHY headline:** Laurel built mediabistro on the value of connecting media people. Orbiter is that thesis with a context graph.

- She spent a decade proving media professionals will pay to find each other, then sold the business that did it. Orbiter automates that same job on a per-tenant context graph, so the network she curated by hand becomes software.
- Her portfolio through Supernode VC leans toward media, community, and network-effect tools. Orbiter is the infrastructure bet beneath all three.
- The round is her natural ticket: a $2.8M NYC seed with a 10,000+ organic waitlist, enterprise pilots, and a founder team that already shipped a category product in Frame.io's orbit.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | Somewhere in the mediabistro years, a freelancer got a career-changing assignment because you built the room where it happened. Orbiter gives every operator that room. |
| `thesis` | You monetized connecting media people before software could. Orbiter does it now on a per-tenant context graph, with 10,000+ operators already waitlisted. |
| `provocative` | mediabistro proved media people will pay to find each other. The dirty secret: they still can't find the right one. We built that. |

### 2. Susan Lyne

**WHY headline:** Susan's career ran on relationship capital from ABC prime time to BBG. Orbiter makes that capital queryable.

- She crossed from running entertainment businesses to funding founders, which is exactly the bridge Orbiter's beachhead sits on: media and entertainment first, venture and dealmakers second.
- BBG Ventures backed women-led consumer companies where distribution was the network. Orbiter turns that kind of network into a durable, owned asset instead of a rented audience.
- She has seen every version of "who you know" decide outcomes for four decades. A platform that remembers everyone and engineers serendipity is the product her career argued for.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | Every career you ever made at ABC started with someone remembering the right person at the right moment. Orbiter exists so nobody has to rely on memory again. |
| `thesis` | You crossed from running prime time at ABC to running BBG Ventures. Our Phase 1 lives exactly on that bridge: media, entertainment, and venture. |
| `provocative` | Entertainment never had an org chart. It had your phone book. We turned that phone book into a graph with six patents under it. |

### 3. Desiree Gruber

**WHY headline:** Desiree has monetized the relationship layer between talent, brands, and media for 30 years. Orbiter sells that layer as software.

- Full Picture runs on knowing who can reach whom and why. Orbiter's double opt-in introduction workflow is her daily practice, automated end to end.
- Her angel book, including Afterparty, Unrivaled, and Phia, is a map of the new talent economy. Orbiter's context graph is the infrastructure all of them eventually need.
- She sits inside the exact persona the product serves: a connector whose value compounds with every introduction she makes.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | You've spent 30 years making introductions that changed people's lives. Orbiter exists so that instinct scales beyond the people lucky enough to know you. |
| `thesis` | Full Picture monetizes the relationship layer between talent, brands and media. Your book (Afterparty, Unrivaled, Phia) is that economy's next form. Orbiter is its infrastructure. |
| `provocative` | The talent business runs on who you can reach. We built the map of who can reach whom, and why. You'll either love it or find it slightly unnerving. |

### 4. Lindsay Kaplan

**WHY headline:** Lindsay proved curated professional networks are a venture-scale business at Chief. Orbiter is the intelligence layer for every network like it.

- Chief's product was the network itself. Orbiter is built on the same premise: the relationships are the asset, and software should make them work harder.
- She watched thousands of executives pay for structured access to each other. Orbiter turns that access into per-member intelligence rather than a directory.
- Her portfolio leans community and women-led consumer. A privacy-first, human-in-the-loop relationship product matches both her thesis and her lived operator experience.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | Chief worked because a woman's network is the one thing nobody could hand her. Orbiter is built on the same belief: the network is the asset. |
| `thesis` | You proved curated professional networks are a venture-scale business. Orbiter is the intelligence layer that makes each member's existing network worth more. |
| `provocative` | Membership was the product at Chief. The product here is what the membership quietly knew all along. |

### 5. Esther Dyson

**WHY headline:** Esther has argued for three decades that reputation and relationships are the real asset. Orbiter finally gives that asset a graph.

- Her angel record, from Delicious and Dopplr to Crowdbooster, is a through-line of tools that make social context usable. Orbiter is the largest version of that bet yet.
- She has written about digital identity, attention, and privacy since before the social web existed. A privacy-first, per-tenant relationship graph with human-in-the-loop outreach is the architecture her positions point to.
- At seed stage she backs the person and the problem. The problem here is the one she has been circling her whole career.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | You've spent a career arguing that who we are to each other matters more than what we do. Orbiter is that conviction, finally executable as software. |
| `thesis` | 75 tracked angel deals with a through-line from Delicious and Dopplr to today: tools that make social context usable. This is the biggest version of that bet yet. |
| `provocative` | Every CRM sells the lie that a relationship is a record. You've known better for decades. We built the alternative. |

### 6. Randi Zuckerberg

**WHY headline:** Randi watched the friend graph become an economy at Facebook. Orbiter is the professional relationship graph's turn.

- She ran marketing at Facebook while the friend graph became the most valuable asset in consumer tech. Orbiter applies the same graph economics to professional relationships, privately and per tenant.
- Her portfolio through Zuckerberg Media and HUG, including Curastory, Virtualness, and Sportsbox AI, sits exactly on the creator-economy and media seam Orbiter opens with.
- A 10,000+ organic waitlist and stealth enterprise pilots is the distribution playbook she ran herself, pointed at a graph she understands better than most.

**Hooks:**

| Angle | Text |
| --- | --- |
| `personal` | You watched the friend graph change what people meant to each other. Orbiter is built for the moment the professional graph does the same. |
| `thesis` | Your book, Curastory, Virtualness, Sportsbox AI, is creator economy at the tech/media seam. Our beachhead is exactly that seam. |
| `provocative` | Facebook made the world searchable by friend. Nobody has made a career searchable by trust. That gap is our $2.8M seed. |

## Load notes

- Resolve all `{entity ID for …}` placeholders through the backend API as described in [Create Demo Suggestions](/guides/open-work/suggestion-delivery-and-tables/create-demo-suggestions#part-2-claude-loads-it). People resolve to `persons.persons.id`; the six `target_org` companies resolve to `companies.companies.id`.
- `hooks` stays a JSONB array on the `suggestion` row. The three `angle` values (`personal`, `thesis`, `provocative`) are demo content, not enum values; the contract only requires `entity_id`, `entity_type`, `angle`, and `text` per hook.
- With empty `action_groups` and `actions`, each card renders WHY plus hooks without outreach buttons. Add `draft_email` actions later if the demo needs the contact arrays.
