## Feature Requirements

Deal Flow v1 gives a person a board of opportunities for one goal, such as raising a seed round or selling to design partners. Each opportunity is a deal about a person or company already in Orbiter. Orbiter's AI keeps deals moving: it notices what has gone quiet, prepares the next step, and asks before it acts.

### Starting point

The backend for this feature is built. The UI is not.

The actions include email drafts and sends, double opt-in introductions and meeting briefs. ### Terminology

The words Deal Flow uses. The backend column maps each term to its name in the built Projects backend, for the table and API review.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Flow | A set of stages that deals move through toward an outcome. A flow belongs to a project and starts from a template or from scratch. Sales tools call this a pipeline. | `projects.pipelines` |
| Stage | One step in a flow and one column on its board. Every stage has a name, a description, requirements to move to the next stage, suggested actions, a stale threshold, and a fixed meaning: backlog, not started, in progress, won or lost. | `projects.pipeline_stages` |

### Shipped templates

Two templates ship with Orbiter, and both are dogfood: Orbiter's own raise and its own enterprise deals run on them first. Each stage lists its meaning, a description, the requirements to move to the next stage, what it adds to the deal's context, and its suggested actions. Both stage lists were approved as written on September 26, 2026 (decision 39).

#### Funding Round Raise

**Outcome:** the round closes and each investor's money arrives. **Each deal:** one investor, an angel or a fund. **Stages:** Targeted, Intro requested, First meeting, Diligence, Partner meeting, Committed, Documents, Signed, then Wired, or Passed from any stage.

Committed, Signed and Wired are separate stages because that is where founders over-count progress. Soft interest, such as a yes that depends on finding a lead, stays in its stage and is recorded apart (DL-12). A lead investor goes through every stage. An angel or follow-on investor usually skips Partner meeting and commits on the lead's terms. In a SAFE round each investor closes separately; a priced round signs and closes together through the lead.

<Steps>
<Step title="Targeted">
**Meaning:** backlog, the entry stage. **Stale after:** no threshold.

The investor has been researched and fits this round.

**To move to Intro requested:**

- Stage, check size and sector fit this round.
- No competing portfolio company.
- The fund is still investing.
- A decision maker is named: a partner, or the angel.
- A path in is chosen: a named connector, or cold outreach.

**Adds to the deal's context:** check-size range, whether they lead, thesis, conflicts, fund timing, paths in and tier.

**Suggested actions:** rank investors by the likelihood of a yes times the value of that yes; find the strongest connector.
</Step>
<Step title="Intro requested">
**Meaning:** not started. **Stale after:** 7 days without a reply.

A warm introduction has been asked for or made, or cold outreach has gone out.

**To move to First meeting:**

- The investor has replied.
- A meeting is on the calendar.

**Adds to the deal's context:** who made the introduction and when, warm or cold, and the reply date.

**Suggested actions:** brief and nudge the connector; follow up after 3 to 4 days, then again 3 to 4 days later. Silence is the most common way investors say no.
</Step>
<Step title="First meeting">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 14 days without a dated next step.

The first pitch to a decision maker.

**To move to Diligence:**

- The meeting happened with a partner or the angel.
- There is a dated next step, or an explicit ask to invest.
- The investor's decision process is recorded.

**Adds to the deal's context:** attendees and titles, process and timeline, objections, what they asked for, lead or follow, and check range.

**Suggested actions:** send the recap and materials the same day; ask what happens next.
</Step>
<Step title="Diligence">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 14 days without a new request.

Follow-up meetings, the data room, and customer and reference calls.

**To move to Partner meeting:**

- Everything requested has been delivered.
- References are supplied and briefed.
- An internal champion books the partner meeting. An angel asks for terms instead.

**Adds to the deal's context:** requests and their status, references called, open questions and the champion. Deck views are not progress.

**Suggested actions:** share the data room; brief references before investors call them.
</Step>
<Step title="Partner meeting">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 2 days without a decision.

The full partnership hears the pitch and decides. Angels skip this stage.

**To move to Committed:**

- An explicit yes from the partnership, or from the angel.

An explicit no moves the deal to Passed. A request for more diligence keeps it here, with a date.

**Adds to the deal's context:** date, attendees, decision and stated reason.

**Suggested actions:** ask when the decision will come. Most funds decide within a day.
</Step>
<Step title="Committed">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 7 days without documents sent.

The founder has accepted a definite offer.

**To move to Documents, one of:**

- Lead investor: the term sheet is signed by both sides.
- SAFE: the exact amount and valuation cap, or uncapped, plus any discount, confirmed in writing with no ranges or conditions.
- Follow-on investor: their allocation on the lead's terms, confirmed in writing.

**Adds to the deal's context:** amount, instrument, valuation or cap and discount, option pool, liquidation preference, board seat, pro rata, the exclusivity end date and the confirming message.

**Suggested actions:** send the documents the same day.
</Step>
<Step title="Documents">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 30 days, or the exclusivity end date.

Lawyers draft the final documents while the investor's confirmatory diligence runs.

**To move to Signed:**

- The documents are final and out for signature.
- Closing conditions are scheduled, such as filings and consents.

**Adds to the deal's context:** lawyers on each side, the documents, the diligence checklist and the target closing date.

**Suggested actions:** keep the data room ready; a ready data room saves about a week.
</Step>
<Step title="Signed">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Stale after:** 10 days without the wire.

Both sides have signed, and wire instructions have gone out.

**To move to Wired:**

- Countersigned documents are in hand.
- Wire details are verified.

**Adds to the deal's context:** signing date, signing entity and when wire instructions were sent.

**Suggested actions:** chase the wire. A SAFE is not binding until it is funded.
</Step>
<Step title="Wired">
**Meaning:** won, the outcome.

The money has arrived.

**To enter Wired:**

- The bank confirms the full amount.
- For a priced round, the charter is filed and escrow is released.
- The cap table is updated.

**Adds to the deal's context:** amount received against amount committed, date received and side letters.

**Suggested actions:** add the investor to investor updates.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Passed** is the lost exit, reachable from any stage. It records the stage the deal left from, who ended it, whether the no was explicit or silent, and the reasons. A "not now" also records the metric or date that would reopen it, and a revisit date.

Proposed pass reasons: wrong stage or check size; outside the thesis; competing portfolio company; fund not investing; market too small; traction too early; team gaps; no internal champion; terms not agreed; no reply.

The stale thresholds for First meeting and Diligence are Orbiter starting points; the others follow the sources. Sources: [YC on seed rounds](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/), [YC Handshake](https://ycombinator.com/handshake), [YC SAFE](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe), [YC diligence checklist](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/3h-series-a-diligence-checklist), [NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/the-non-obvious-guide-to-fundraising), [CRV on passes](https://www.crv.com/content/why-investors-pass), [CRV on term sheets](https://www.crv.com/content/term-sheet), [First Round](https://review.firstround.com/heres-what-you-can-really-expect-when-pitching-your-seed-stage-startup-at-a-vc-partner-meeting/), [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/fr.html), [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-how-to-handle-investor-rejection-turning-no-into-future-yes), [Hustle Commons](https://www.hustlecommons.com/blog/how-to-follow-up-after-your-first-pitch), [Visible](https://visible.vc/product/pipelines/), [on soft commitments](https://www.admnt.com/blog/soft-commitments-they-suck-why-when-and-how).

#### Enterprise Sale

**Outcome:** a signed contract. **Each deal:** one opportunity with one enterprise customer: a sale with several stakeholders, a security review and procurement. The target is usually the company, though a person can be the target; the people involved join as deal people. **Stages:** Target account, Discovery, Qualification, Solution fit, Technical validation, Business case and proposal, Security, legal and procurement, Verbal commit, then Closed won, or Closed lost from any stage.

Requirements are things the buyer did, not seller activity, and they accumulate from stage to stage. A deal moves back only with a named reason, such as champion lost, budget frozen or evaluation paused (FL-20). Target account is an Orbiter addition, so sourced accounts have somewhere to land; the research starts at Discovery. Typical win probabilities are kept for weighted values later (FL-09).

<Steps>
<Step title="Target account">
**Meaning:** backlog, the entry stage. **Stale after:** no threshold.

An account that fits the ideal customer profile and has not engaged yet.

**To move to Discovery:**

- The account fits the ideal customer profile.
- A first contact or a warm path in is identified.
- A first meeting is booked.

**Adds to the deal's context:** fit notes, the path in and the source.

**Suggested actions:** find the strongest warm path; request an introduction.
</Step>
<Step title="Discovery">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 10%. **Stale after:** 7 days with nothing scheduled.

First meetings to learn the situation, the pain, its impact and why now.

**To move to Qualification:**

- The buyer confirmed a written recap of pain, stakeholders, next steps and timeline.
- A critical event, the buyer's own deadline, is identified.
- The economic buyer is named, or a path to them is identified.
- A scoping meeting is booked.

**Adds to the deal's context:** situation, pain, impact, critical event and decision; attendees and roles; the status quo and alternatives. Pain and competition start the MEDDPICC record.

**Suggested actions:** send the recap; raise budget early; map the stakeholders.
</Step>
<Step title="Qualification">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 25%. **Stale after:** 7 days with nothing scheduled.

The buyer confirms this is a funded priority and that doing nothing has a cost.

**To move to Solution fit:**

- The economic buyer is named, and the champion agrees to arrange a meeting with them.
- The buyer has quantified baseline and target metrics.
- Decision criteria and the decision process, including steps, people, dates and any veto, are documented and played back to the champion.
- Competition is listed, including doing nothing.
- The budget source and the critical-event date are confirmed.

**Adds to the deal's context:** the MEDDPICC fields with their evidence; why now, why do anything and why us; the buying group, often 5 to 16 people.

**Suggested actions:** ask for the economic buyer meeting; draft the mutual action plan; walk away if the gaps persist.
</Step>
<Step title="Solution fit">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 40%. **Stale after:** 7 days with nothing scheduled.

A demo tailored to the decision criteria, for the wider buying group.

**To move to Technical validation:**

- The economic buyer is engaged.
- Several people at the buyer are engaged, not just one contact.
- The buyer confirms the required capabilities are met.
- The champion is tested: they did something costly for the deal, such as arranging access or sharing internal information.
- Written proof-of-concept success criteria, an owner and an end date are agreed, or the buyer waives the proof of concept.
- The MEDDPICC record is complete.

**Adds to the deal's context:** a decision-criteria scorecard, attendees by role, the champion-test evidence and the proof-of-concept plan.

**Suggested actions:** send the security pack before it is asked for; scope the proof of concept; share the draft action plan.
</Step>
<Step title="Technical validation">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 60%. **Stale after:** the agreed end date.

A time-boxed proof of concept or pilot against the agreed criteria.

**To move to Business case and proposal:**

- The buyer's technical owner signs off that the criteria were met.
- The ROI measures are validated.
- Both sides agree to move to a proposal.
- The contracting steps are mapped: legal, security, procurement and signature.
- The security questionnaire is done or underway.

**Adds to the deal's context:** criteria against results, and the security documents requested.

**Suggested actions:** hold a results readout with the economic buyer; share the SOC 2 report under NDA; draft the business case. A technical win alone does not guarantee a deal.
</Step>
<Step title="Business case and proposal">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 75%. **Stale after:** 7 days with nothing scheduled, or any push of the close date.

A quantified business case and a commercial proposal go to the economic buyer.

**To move to Security, legal and procurement:**

- The economic buyer agrees the business case.
- The buyer names the seller as the selected vendor.
- A close plan with dates through signature and go-live is reviewed with the champion, the economic buyer, finance and purchasing.
- The procurement process and implementation plan are confirmed.

**Adds to the deal's context:** proposal versions, the close plan and any RFP status.

**Suggested actions:** send the master agreement and data processing agreement as editable documents; start the buyer's procurement intake.
</Step>
<Step title="Security, legal and procurement">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 90%. **Stale after:** 30 days.

The buyer runs security, privacy, legal and procurement reviews and supplier onboarding, often in parallel.

**To move to Verbal commit:**

- The security review is approved, with any exceptions written down.
- A data processing agreement is signed where personal data is processed.
- Business and legal terms are agreed, and the paperwork is ready to route.
- The authorized signatory and the signature date are confirmed.

**Adds to the deal's context:** the owner, status and date of each review; a redline log; discount approvals; whether a purchase order is required.

**Suggested actions:** turn redlines around fast; register as a supplier early. Legal often takes one to three weeks, each security or privacy review 4 to 14 days, and procurement 2 to 4 weeks.
</Step>
<Step title="Verbal commit">
**Meaning:** in progress. **Typical win probability:** 95%. **Stale after:** 10 business days.

Terms are agreed verbally and the documents are final.

**To move to Closed won:**

- The documents are fully signed.
- A purchase order is issued, if one is required.
- Supplier registration is complete.
- The go-live plan and success metrics are handed to onboarding.

**Adds to the deal's context:** signed documents, annual and total contract value, term, signatories, purchase order and go-live date.

**Suggested actions:** chase signatures and the purchase order; book the kickoff.
</Step>
<Step title="Closed won">
**Meaning:** won, the outcome.

The contract is signed and approved.
</Step>
</Steps>

**Closed lost** is the lost exit, reachable from any stage. It records the reasons, the stage at loss, notes and a re-engagement plan, and a competitive loss names the competitor. No decision is the most common loss: sources put it at 40 to 60% of deals.

Proposed loss reasons: no decision, preferring the status quo; no decision, unable to choose; competitor; budget or priority; no economic buyer; champion left; failed validation; security, legal or procurement blocker; price or terms; critical event passed.

| Competition | Discovery | Qualification, revisited in Business case and proposal |

### How teams use deal flow today

A September 2026 review of practitioner and research sources on how founders, investors and sales teams run pipelines. Each row ends with what it asks of Deal Flow. Sources marked vendor come from a company that sells the tool.

#### Founders raising a round

| Practice | What it asks of Deal Flow | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talk to many investors in parallel, tiered by likelihood and check size, and practise on lower tiers first. A round can take dozens of pitches. | A tier on each deal (DL-07) and a board that holds a long list. | [Paul Graham](https://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html), [NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/nfx-fundraising-manual), [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-funding) |
| Keep the tracker simple: status, who owns each intro, objections and notes, updated daily. | A light deal record with an intro owner, and no setup before the first deal (NF-06). | [NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/nfx-fundraising-manual), [folk, vendor](https://www.folk.app/articles/how-using-our-own-product-helped-us-streamline-our-fundraising-process) |
| Batch meetings against deadlines so interest and offers arrive together. | A raise view with phase deadlines and meetings per week (RP-05). | [NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/nfx-fundraising-manual), [YC Series A guide, summarized by Visible](https://visible.vc/blog/y-combinator-series-a/) |

#### Pain points across all three

- **Nobody trusts hand-entered data.** In a 2025 Validity survey, 76% of respondents said less than half of their CRM data was accurate and complete ([Validity](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/validity-releases-state-crm-data-130000354.html)). Investor teams report hours of CRM upkeep every week ([Affinity, vendor](https://www.affinity.co/blog/pipeline-management)). Capture has to be automatic.
- **Interest is misread, and passes are silent.** Founders hear yes when it was maybe, and slow or patchy replies usually mean a pass ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/nfx-fundraising-manual), [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/why-investors-pass)). Stale should mean silence from the other side.
- **The list runs dry.** Keeping the top of the funnel full for months was Elizabeth Yin's main lesson after 345 meetings ([Elizabeth Yin](https://elizabethyin.com/2018/12/19/how-i-raised-my-11-5m-vc-fund/)). Sourcing suggestions (AI-12) feed the first stage.
- **Too many tools and nudges.** Independent evidence on which nudges people ignore is thin, and claims about alert fatigue come only from vendors. Keep nudges few, explained and capped (AI-23).
- **Cost shuts out solo users.** Investor CRMs start at about $20K a year, too much for angels and solo founders ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/affinity-crm-for-venture-capital-a-founder-and-vcs-honest-review)). A light entry point matters (NF-06).

What this means for Deal Flow v1:

- **Much of the "AI" nudging is rules.** Pipedrive states its [Pulse feed](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pulse-feed) uses fixed rules and time triggers. Rules pick the deals; a model drafts and explains.
- **Approval screens have converged.** Current value, proposed value, the source and approval one field at a time. No silent overwrites.
- **Stage checks usually stop at the UI.** Pipedrive's required fields are not enforced on imports, bulk edits, API calls or automations. Orbiter's backend already runs the same checks for an agent's move as for a drag.
- **Relationships separate investor CRMs from sales CRMs.** Orbiter's graph, warm paths and How I Know records are the ground to build on.

### How to read the requirements

- **ID** gives each requirement a stable name for workshop notes and decisions.
- **Proposed** is `v1` or `Later`. It is a proposal for the workshop, not a decision.
- **API today** compares the requirement with the built Projects API: **Built**, **Partial**, **Not yet** or **UI only**. Table and API changes are decided after the requirements are agreed.

### 12. AI-assisted deal progression

The AI's job is to keep every open deal moving: notice what has stalled, prepare the next step, and make acting on it one click.

#### Rules for every AI suggestion

| Rule | What it means | API today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI proposes; a person decides | No stage move, send or close happens without a person's explicit action. | Built: reviews write proposals only; each action needs exact authorization. |
| Every suggestion says why | The rationale quotes the evidence: a stage, a date, a count, an email. | Built: a rationale is required. |
| One idea is one suggestion | Dismissing an idea stops it returning in new words. | Built: suggestions are keyed by intent. |
| Few suggestions | At most five open per deal, and none when nothing is warranted. | Built. |

### Suggestions

1. **Build on the Projects backend.** Its pipelines, checklists, board, suggestions and actions already cover most rows above. [Deal Flow Tables](/guides/open-work/deal-flow-v1/deal-flow-tables) should then record only additions.
2. **Open the tab on a flow, not on projects.** Every deal belongs to a project, and the backend rules out a hidden automatic project. A first run can ask what the user is working on, then create the project and a flow from a template together, such as "Seed round" from Funding Round Raise.
3. **Make a dated next step the habit that holds the product together.** Show it on every card, flag deals without one, and make keeping it current the AI's first job.
4. **Give the review each viewer's own context, privately.** Email, calendar and relationship context produce the most useful suggestions. They stay visible only to the person whose data they came from.
5. **Let the AI propose stage moves.** A one-click confirmation runs the same checklist as a drag. Nothing moves on its own.
6. **Reuse existing surfaces.** The person and company side panels, the [Contacts Table View](/guides/open-work/contacts-table-view) pattern for the table view, the [Warm Intro](/guides/open-work/warm-intro-feature) workspace for introductions, and the Hot Keys registry. The existing Deal Flow tab and command palette entry become the entry points.
7. **Add source and tier to deals from the start.** Later reporting on what works depends on them.
8. **Let rules pick the deals and the model write.** Plain triggers choose what needs attention: stale, close date pushed, single-threaded, unanswered message. The model drafts and explains. Suggestions stay predictable and within budget, and this matches how the backend already selects deals for review.
9. **Use the approval screen other tools have converged on** for every AI-proposed field change: current value, proposed value, source, approve per field.
10. **Lead with relationships.** Warm paths and How I Know on every card are what investor CRMs sell and sales CRMs lack.
11. **Capture activity instead of asking for it.** Email and calendar fill the timeline, the last contact and the next meeting, with controls to leave personal mail out (AC-02, AC-07). Hand entry is the top complaint in all three workflows.
12. **Define stale as silence from the other side** (AC-06), tuned per stage. Editing the deal should not reset the clock.
13. **Treat a pass as a state with a future.** "Not now" carries a revisit date and can join an update list; "no" closes the deal.
14. **Keep fundraising honest.** Committed, Signed and Wired are separate stages and soft interest is recorded apart (DL-12), so the round tracker never counts a maybe (RP-02).
15. **Build the weekly review in** (RP-06). Sales and investor teams already hold one, usually from a spreadsheet.
16. **Dogfood both templates on Orbiter's own work.** Run Orbiter's next raise in Funding Round Raise and its enterprise deals in Enterprise Sale. Real deals test the stage requirements, the stage context and the AI suggestions before customers rely on them.
